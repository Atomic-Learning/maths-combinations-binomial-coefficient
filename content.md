A combination is a selection of items from a larger pool, where the order of selection does not matter. For example, if a pool contains items (A, B, C), the possible combinations of two items are AB, AC, and BC. AB and BA are considered the same combination.

# The Binomial Coefficient

The binomial coefficient, denoted as $\binom{n}{k}$ or $C(n, k)$ (often spoken as "n choose k"), represents the number of combinations when k items are chosen from a pool of n items without regard to the order of selection. It is calculated using the formula:

$$\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}$$

For example, if n = 3 and k = 2, the number of combinations is:

$$\binom{3}{2} = \frac{3!}{2!(3-2)!} = \frac{6}{2 \cdot 1} = 3$$

This matches our earlier example where the possible combinations of two items from a pool of three items (A, B, C) are AB, AC, and BC.
